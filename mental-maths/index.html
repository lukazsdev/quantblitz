The mental maths cheat sheet for trading interviews

Optiver gives you 8 minutes for 80 questions. Flow Traders gives you 10 for 75. Nobody passes those by calculating faster. They pass by recognising patterns. These are the patterns.

1Multiplication

  • Difference of squares: (a − b)(a + b) = a² − b² 47 × 53 = 50² − 3² = 2500 − 9 = 2491. Any pair the same distance from a round number.
  • Squares near 50: (50 + n)² = 2500 + 100n + n² 53² = 2500 + 300 + 9 = 2809. 46² = 2500 − 400 + 16 = 2116.
  • Squares near 100: (100 + n)² = 10000 + 200n + n² 104² = 10000 + 800 + 16 = 10816. 97² = 10000 − 600 + 9 = 9409.
  • Ending in 5: n5² = n(n + 1) then 25 85² → 8 × 9 = 72 → 7225. 115² → 11 × 12 = 132 → 13225.
  • Times 11: spread the digits and add neighbours 36 × 11 = 3 (3 + 6) 6 = 396. With a carry: 78 × 11 = 7 (15) 8 → 858.
  • Times 5, 25, 125: halve, quarter, eighth, then add zeros 348 × 5 = 174 × 10 = 1740. 88 × 25 = 22 × 100 = 2200. 56 × 125 = 7 × 1000 = 7000.
  • Times 9, 99, 999: times 10 (100, 1000) minus the number 47 × 99 = 4700 − 47 = 4653. 23 × 999 = 23000 − 23 = 22977.
  • Round and correct 19 × 24 = 20 × 24 − 24 = 456. 31 × 42 = 30 × 42 + 42 = 1302.
  • Two digits by two digits: the cross method 23 × 47: 2×4 = 8 | 2×7 + 3×4 = 26 | 3×7 = 21 → 800 + 260 + 21 = 1081. Say the hundreds first, then the cross, then the units.

2Division and fractions

  • Divide by 5: double, then move the point 347 ÷ 5 = 694 ÷ 10 = 69.4. Divide by 25: times 4, then ÷ 100.
  • Divisibility By 3 or 9: digit sum. By 4: last two digits. By 8: last three. By 11: alternating digit sum is 0 or a multiple of 11.
  • Know these as decimals cold They appear in every test, as fractions, percentages and reciprocals.

3Percentages

  • a% of b = b% of a 8% of 25 = 25% of 8 = 2. 16% of 50 = 50% of 16 = 8.
  • Build from 10%, 5% and 1% 17% of 240 = 24 + 12 + 4.8 = 40.8. 35% of 80 = 24 + 4 = 28.
  • Up x% then down x% is not zero (1 + x)(1 − x) = 1 − x². Up 20% then down 20% leaves 0.96: a 4% loss.
  • Rule of 72 Years to double ≈ 72 ÷ rate. At 6% a year, about 12 years. At 9%, 8.
  • Reverse percentages Price after 25% off is 60: original = 60 ÷ 0.75 = 80. Never add the percentage back.

4Estimation

  • Squares to 30, cubes to 10, powers of 2 to 2¹⁶ 17² = 289, 23² = 529, 27² = 729, 29² = 841. 7³ = 343, 9³ = 729. 2¹⁰ = 1024 ≈ 10³, 2¹⁶ = 65,536.
  • Square roots: √n ≈ a + (n − a²) ÷ 2a, with a the nearest perfect square's root √50 ≈ 7 + 1/14 ≈ 7.07. √120 ≈ 11 − 1/22 ≈ 10.95. √2 ≈ 1.414, √3 ≈ 1.732, √5 ≈ 2.236.
  • Constants that get asked e ≈ 2.718, π ≈ 3.1416, ln 2 ≈ 0.693, log₁₀ 2 ≈ 0.301, log₁₀ 3 ≈ 0.477. So 2²⁰ ≈ 10⁶ and 1000 ≈ 2¹⁰.
  • Fermi questions: factor, round, count the zeros Tube journeys a day in London? ~9m people × 0.5 journeys ≈ 4 to 5 million. Keep every factor to one significant figure and track the powers of ten separately.

5How to play the test

  • With negative marking, accuracy beats coverage Optiver-style tests subtract for wrong answers. A question you can't see within 3 seconds is a skip, not a guess.
  • Compute with integers, place the decimal point last 0.36 × 2.5: do 36 × 25 = 900, then count three decimal places: 0.9.
  • On multiple choice, eliminate by magnitude and last digit 47 × 53 ends in 1 and is about 2500. Only one option survives, often before you finish the sum.
  • Train in 5-minute timed rounds, every day Speed comes from recall, not effort. Twenty minutes once a week does far less than five minutes daily.
